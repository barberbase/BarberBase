Walk-in salons lose customers in one specific way: someone joins the line, gets bored waiting in a physical room with no idea how long is left, and leaves. They don't come back that day, and the chair sits empty until the next walk-in.

The fix isn't a better waiting room. It's not needing to wait in it.

## The mechanism

BarberBase replaces the physical line with a queue a customer joins from their own phone — no app, no account. They scan a QR code or tap a link, and they're in. From that point on, three things happen automatically:

1. **Live position and estimated wait**, visible the moment they join and updating in real time as the queue moves — no refreshing, no asking staff "how much longer?"
2. **A WhatsApp message when they're close** — the same channel they already use, not a notification from an app they had to install for one visit.
3. **An "on my way" tap when they head back in, then a 4-digit PIN posted at the shop counter to confirm they've actually arrived** — so staff know a customer is genuinely returning and not just testing whether they can hold a spot indefinitely.

None of this requires the customer to stand in the shop. They can wait next door, run an errand, or sit in their car — and they will, because the alternative (standing in a waiting room hoping for the best) is worse for everyone.

## Why WhatsApp specifically

The channel matters as much as the mechanism. A native app requires a download for a five-minute interaction, and most people won't do it. Email gets buried. SMS costs more per message and delivers a plain-text notification with none of the formatting that makes a queue update easy to read at a glance.

WhatsApp is already installed, already open, and already the channel Mumbai salons use to talk to regulars. Routing queue updates through it means the notification lands somewhere the customer is actually going to see it.

## What this changes on the shop side

Staff run one dashboard: call the next customer, start the service, mark it complete, add a walk-in, or assign a specific barber. The same real-time state that updates the customer's phone updates the staff view — nobody is relaying information between a paper list and a phone screen.

Appointments work the same way. A customer picks a service and a time slot, and gets a WhatsApp confirmation — no back-and-forth, no phone tag.

The result is a queue that behaves the way waiting already works in a customer's head: they know where they stand, they get told when it matters, and they don't have to stand in a room to find out.
